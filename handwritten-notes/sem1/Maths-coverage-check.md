## 5. Content errors found and fixed (previous worker's drafts)

1. **Jul-2022 B7 — Charpit, `y p = 2 y x + log q`:** the draft said "solve by putting p = a". Wrong. Correct solution: Charpit gives p = 2x + a, q = e^{a y}, complete integral z = x² + a x + e^{a y}/a + b (verified by substitution). Fixed in `maths_content.py`; the Jul-2022 solution shows the correct working and explains why p = a fails.
2. **2024 C4 — "Removal of the first derivative":** the draft claimed I = −1/4 (constant) for y'' + (3 sin x − cot x)y' + 2 sin²x·y = e^{−cos x} sin²x. Wrong: I = (1/4)(cos²x − 3 cosec²x), not constant (at x = π/2 it is −3/4). The paragraph now says so and points to the correct method. The correct worked example is in "Change of independent variable": put z = cos x → y = C1 e^{cos x} + C2 e^{2 cos x} + (1/6) e^{−cos x} (verified by substitution).
3. **Jan-2020 A4 — a₀ of |x| on (−π, π):** the draft's entry answered a different question (alternating harmonic series → log 2). Replaced with the correct answer: a₀ = (1/π)∫_{−π}^{π}|x| dx = π, so the Fourier series begins π/2 − (4/π)Σ cos((2n−1)x)/(2n−1)².
4. **Mar-2021 C4(b) — continuity of f(x, y) = xy³ (f(0,0) = 0):** the draft's box claimed xy³/(x²+y²) is discontinuous. As printed the function is xy³ with no denominator, and it IS continuous at (0,0) (|xy³| ≤ r⁴ → 0). Entry rewritten: proves continuity as printed and shows the intended discontinuous example xy³/(x²+y⁶) (Dec-2019 A5).
5. **Asymptotes example factorisation:** corrected to x³ + 2x²y − xy² − 2y³ = (x − y)(x + y)(x + 2y).
6. Minor wording fixes in the final rebuild: the "intersections at infinity" mistake note (it contradicted the worked example — a point at infinity counts when the given line passes through it); the Euler-theorem proof sentence; the reduction-of-order paragraph ("..." shorthand removed); a duplicated "Exam Tip: Exam Tip:" in the Dec-2019 Cauchy-sequence answer.

## 6. Flagged questions (misprinted or unclear in the scans)

Each flag also appears at the end of that paper's solution PDF.

- **EM1-Dec-2018, B4:** the paper says f(x, y) = xy/√(x²+y²) "is not continuous at (0,0)". False — |f| ≤ (1/2)√(x²+y²) → 0, so it IS continuous. The answer proves the partial derivatives exist (both 0) and notes the misprint; the classic discontinuous example is xy/(x²+y²).
- **EM1-Jan-2020, A8:** as printed, ∫₀^b∫₀^x xy dx dy is inconsistent (inner limit x is not a variable of the outer integral). Solved as ∫₀^b∫₀^y xy dx dy = b⁴/8.
- **EM1-Jan-2020, B6:** printed limits unclear in the scan (inner integral appears to run from x to ∞). Solved as ∫₀^∞∫ₓ^∞ e^{−y}/y dy dx = 1.
- **EM1-Jan-2020, C6:** printed order of differentials unclear in the scan. Solved as ∫₀^{log 2}∫₀^x∫₀^{x+log y} e^{x+y+z} dz dy dx = (8/3) log 2 − 19/9.
- **EM1-Jul-2022, B3:** asymptotes are y = x/2 − 1/4, y = −x/2 − 3/4, y = x/3 + 1/3, y = −x/3 + 2/3. Only (−1, 0) of the re-intersections lies on x²+y² = 1 (the others are (−7, −2), two irrational points and two complex points). The "circle of radius unity" claim is an apparent misprint (probably in the degree-2 terms). Worked out in full in the solution.
- **EM1-Jul-2022, C5:** RHS as printed reads (l²+m²) d²z/dy² + d²z/dv²; the correct statement proved is (l²+m²)(d²z/du² + d²z/dv²).
- **EM1-Mar-2021, A8:** inner limit reads "3y", impossible for an integral over y. Solved as ∫₁²∫₀³ y dy dx = 9/2.
- **EM1-Mar-2021, C2:** the identity as printed (π/sinh π = 2[1/(2²+1) + 1/(3²+1) + …]) is misprinted. Correct: π/sinh(aπ) = 1/a + 2a Σ(−1)ⁿ/(a²+n²), giving π/sinh π = 2[1/5 − 1/10 + 1/17 − …] for a = 1.
- **EM1-Mar-2021, C4(b):** as printed, f(x, y) = xy³ (f(0,0) = 0) IS continuous at (0,0); see fix 4 above.
- **EM1-May-2019, A4:** "Give an example of a divergent series whose sum is convergent" is self-contradictory as worded. Answered as the intended meaning: a conditionally convergent series, e.g. the alternating harmonic series Σ(−1)^{n+1}/n = log 2 (converges, but Σ1/n diverges).
- **EM1-May-2019, B4:** the point (1, 2, 3) is NOT on xyz = 4 (1·2·3 = 6). Solved for xyz = 6 at (1, 2, 3), and also gives the (1, 2, 2) case on xyz = 4.

Not flagged, but worth noting: **EM1-2025, C2** (x²y'' − xy' − y = x²e^x) is genuinely non-elementary — the variation-of-parameters integrals ∫ e^x x^{±√2} dx are incomplete-gamma functions. The solution gives the complementary function and the variation-of-parameters setup with the particular integral in integral form, and says so explicitly.
